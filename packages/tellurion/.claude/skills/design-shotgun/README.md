# Design Shotgun

_Generate 3-5 fundamentally different design directions with structured pros/cons, then converge on the winner using comparison data._

**CATEGORY** Skills · Design  •  **TRIGGER** `design shotgun`  •  **RATING** ★★★★☆ (8/10)  •  **STATUS** Curated

---

## Why It Earns the Curated Bar

- **Breaks single-solution bias**: Generates breadth-first alternatives instead of depth-first perfection of one idea.
- **Structured comparison prevents false choice**: Produces pros/cons, tradeoffs, effort, and risk for each direction—decisions based on data, not preference.
- **Converges on winner rationally**: Comparison tables make the best direction obvious; team aligns without debate.

## What It Does

This skill rapidly explores design space by generating 3-5 fundamentally different directions and comparing them structurally. It starts by understanding the brief: what's being designed (page, component, layout), constraints (brand colors, existing system), audience (operators vs. consumers), emotional tone, and competitive context.

Then it generates three to five distinct directions that differ in their core approach—not minor tweaks. For example, for a dashboard: Direction 1 emphasizes metric-first KPI layout, Direction 2 favors table-centric data exploration, Direction 3 prioritizes visualization-first analysis. Each direction is visually distinct, not color variations of the same layout.

For each direction, the skill documents: pros (what it does well), cons (what it does poorly), tradeoffs (what you sacrifice for this approach), effort (implementation complexity), and risk (dependencies, browser support, performance implications). This data goes into a structured comparison table so the team can weigh tradeoffs rationally. The skill then facilitates convergence by highlighting which direction best balances the priorities (speed vs.

richness, learnable vs. powerful, mobile vs. desktop). The output is a clear winner and documented reasoning for the choice.

## How to Use

1. **Define the brief**: Specify what's being designed, constraints, audience, emotional target, and competitive context.
2. **Generate directions**: Call design-shotgun to produce 3-5 alternatives.
3. **Review comparison table**: Study pros, cons, effort, and risk for each direction.
4. **Weight priorities**: Identify which tradeoffs matter most for your product.
5. **Converge**: Use the data to select the winning direction; document the reasoning.
6. **Hand off to design-system-architect or interface-design**: Proceed with the chosen direction.

## What NOT to Do

- Do not generate minor color or spacing variations—shotgun alternatives must differ fundamentally in approach.
- Do not skip the structured comparison phase; comparison tables are non-negotiable for rational decision-making.
- Do not treat effort/risk as secondary—a beautiful direction that's expensive to build is still expensive.
- Do not generate directions without understanding constraints; every alternative must be technically and commercially viable.
