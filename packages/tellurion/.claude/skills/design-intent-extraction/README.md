# Design Intent Extraction

_Extract design principles from references and remap them through your product context._

**CATEGORY** Skills · Design  •  **TRIGGER** `design reference`  •  **RATING** ★★★★☆ (8/10)  •  **STATUS** Curated

---

## Why It Earns the Curated Bar

- **Prevents pixel-for-pixel cargo cult copying**: Extracts principles (spacing logic, hierarchy strategy, density approach) instead of creating look-alikes that fail context validation.
- **Contextualizes references through classification**: Maps extracted principles through your Classification Card to respect product tenancy, target audience, and density constraints.
- **Produces design Intent Document, not a clone**: Outputs rules and patterns (e.g., "4-8-16 spacing scale," "hero + supporting type") that inform your design without visual plagiarism.

## What It Does

When you share a reference image or template, this skill extracts its underlying design PRINCIPLES instead of its pixels. It analyzes seven dimensions: spacing system (base grid, padding-to-gap ratios), typography hierarchy (relative sizes, weight distribution), color strategy (semantic roles, accent placement), density approach (cards-per-view, whitespace ratios), layout logic (grid system, responsive breakpoints), interaction model (patterns for navigation, modals, forms), and visual finish (shadows, borders, borders, micro-interactions).

Each dimension is analyzed with specific measurements and evidence—not impressions. For example, "spacing system: base unit 8px; card padding 16px; gap between cards 24px; ratio = 1:3" instead of "good spacing." The skill then reads your Classification Card and remaps these principles to your context.

If your reference is a premium SaaS app but your classification is "lightweight," the skill adjusts density downward. If your reference uses a 12-column grid but your app is mobile-first, it remaps to a flexible approach.

The output is a Design Intent Document listing extracted principles and their application rules for YOUR product—not instructions to copy the reference. This prevents the common failure where a beautiful Stripe or Arc app becomes a terrible clone when applied wholesale to a different product category.

## How to Use

1. **Share one or more reference images**: Include landing pages, dashboards, components, or full apps that inspire direction.
2. **Optionally provide Classification Card**: If already written, the skill remaps principles immediately; otherwise, you provide context.
3. **Skill analyzes each reference**: Extracts spacing, type, color, density, layout, interaction, and finish principles with measurements.
4. **Review Design Intent Document**: Verify that principles are accurate and appropriately contextualized for your product.
5. **Use as design constraints**: Pass the document to creative-director, interface-design, or design-system-architect for implementation.

## What NOT to Do

- Do not share a reference expecting pixel-for-pixel output—the skill produces principles, not replicas.
- Do not skip Classification Card context—principles remapped to wrong product type will still fail context validation.
- Do not treat extracted spacing ratios as absolute; if your design system differs, adapt them.
- Do not extract principles from products in unrelated categories and expect them to transfer directly.
